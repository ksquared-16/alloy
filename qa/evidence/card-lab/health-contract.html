The finding that matters most

Three Forms subsystems, two answers about where child health binds.

systemFieldRegistry.ts registers allergy_notes and medication_flag at entity_type: "enrollment". canonicalBindingSuggestions.ts suggests cm("allergies") and cm("medical_notes") — where cm is entity_type: "customer_member", scope: "child". And packets/sharedValuesToFieldIds.ts uses customer_member:allergies as its canonical key example.

Two of three point at the child; the one that ships the registered fields points at the enrollment. An operator authoring a form is suggested a child-grain destination that has no registered field, while the registered field lands on the enrollment episode — so a child's allergy does not survive their enrollment. Migration M1 must land before either lane binds more health.

Correction to the brief

The brief states physician/dentist relationships have already been registered. Fetched origin/staging (tip 9aad66349, Aug 23): RELATIONSHIP_DEFINITIONS contains exactly three roles — guardian, emergency_contact, authorized_pickup. Physician and dentist are not registered in code.

This is not a blocker. person_child_relationship is child-grain (customer_member_id + person_id) with an open role vocabulary — OperationalRoleKey = PersonChildOperationalRoleKey | (string & {}), documented as “a relationship definition can declare a role the platform has never heard of (physician, attorney, case worker)”, and the resolver is explicitly “generic for ANY configured role … no per-role code”. Physician and dentist are configuration, not code. If Enrollment created them as org config that is correct and complete; if it expected code, none exists and none is needed.

Minimal foundation — one entity, four kinds

person_health_facts with a fact_kind discriminator (allergy | condition | medication | immunization), polymorphic by subject_entity_type/subject_entity_id exactly as documents already is, with the payload schema org-configurable per kind — so no childcare conditional ever reaches the code.

Why one and not three: the card, the requirement evaluator and the packet planner all want one list per child. Three tables triple the resolver, provider, proposal adapter and RLS surface for shapes that share everything but the payload, and a medication referencing the allergy it treats would need a polymorphic join.

Versioning is supersede, never delete — a correction writes a new row with supersedes_id and closes the old one; a resolution just closes it. This mirrors attendance's original | correction | reversal, so the platform keeps one mental model for “a durable fact changed”.

Build order H1 → H2 → H3 → H4 (entity → collection provider registration → resolver → mutation capabilities). Forms binding and Trust proposals both start working the moment H2 lands, because both already speak the collection-provider contract.

Immunization — one fact per vaccine

Immunization is vaccine → doses[] → date — two levels of nesting, which is exactly why 69 CIS destinations compress to ~15 semantic needs. One fact per vaccine, with the dose series in the payload. Doses are ordered values of one fact, not independent facts — nothing references a single dose — and one row per vaccine keeps the collection grain uniform, so no immunization special case reaches the resolver. vaccine_key and the required series come from org configuration, which is what makes Oregon's schedule differ without a jurisdiction branch.

Exemption is not a dose and not a payload field. It is a requirement exception with its own approval and evidence. Burying it in vaccine data makes “no doses recorded” and “lawfully exempt” indistinguishable. Requirement exceptions are not modelled today → cross-sprint decision.

The CIS itself stays documents evidence — never immunization_document_present = true. Processing already classifies immunization_record as a document type.

Do not route everything through AI. A closed choice bound to a registered field must take the deterministic path — interpretation adds cost, latency and a failure mode for no gain. Participant Runtime type and plausibility checks stay upstream of every path.

Migration findings

  1. M1Wrong grain. Re-binding health fields to customer_member moves existing field_values rows between entity grains, and any form bound to the enrollment-grain field needs its field_source updated. Reversible, but it touches tenant data. Do it before either lane binds more health.
  2. M2allergy_notes free text is not mechanically migratable. A blob cannot be split into allergen/severity/reaction by rule. Route it through Trust as a one-time interpretation pass with operator approval — and do not delete the note until its facts are approved.
  3. M3medication_flag should be deprecated, not migrated. “This child takes medication” is derivable from the collection once H1 lands; keeping both creates two answers to one question.

Six decisions

  1. 1M1 — approve the grain migration. Everything else assumes it.
  2. 2Immunization exemption — requirement exception, health fact, or process state? Recommend requirement exception; exceptions are not modelled today.
  3. 3Emergency medical authorization — a consent must carry the version authorized, by whom, when, and whether withdrawn. A form signature proves a signature, not withdrawal. Recommend a canonical consent record, scoped separately.
  4. 4Safeguarding / custody — needs an owner, and it is not Health. Highest sensitivity of anything here, and it must not land in Health merely because it sits near health questions in the source documents.
  5. 5One entity, four kinds, immunization included with a dose series in the payload — confirm.
  6. 6S2 — health visibility as a field-level permission, before any Safety Signal ships.
