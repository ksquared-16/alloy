# The Shared Understanding Model

*The living engineering state Director and the operator build together — what it is, what it contains, how it forms, changes, converges, and becomes strong enough to rely on.*

This is the authoritative product model for the thing an engagement actually produces. It sits within the immutable doctrine — the **Constitution of Engineering Leadership** (who Director is), the **Engineering Partnership Model** (how the relationship feels), and the **Leadership Intelligence Model** (how Director thinks) — and does not restate them. It contains no implementation, code, schemas, APIs, prompts, provider behavior, or interface design. It is only the product model.

---

## The reframe, and the one idea

For most of Vacilando's life the primary artifact was the **Mission Package** — a thing assembled *before* execution and handed off. That framing is now wrong in a specific way: it treats the work as a document to be completed, when the work is actually an *understanding to be developed*. Facts get discovered, assumptions get exposed, intent shifts, risks sharpen, decisions get made and revisited, readiness gets *earned*. None of that is "filling in a package." All of it is building a shared grasp of the work solid enough to act on.

So the deliverable of an engagement is not a package. It is a **Shared Understanding**. And the single idea that governs this whole model:

> **Director does not manufacture an authoritative artifact. Director helps the operator build, challenge, revise, and eventually rely upon a sufficiently strong understanding of the work — and the durable record exists only to make that understanding legible, testable, and shared, never to replace it.**

Everything below follows from taking that seriously.

---

## 1. The fundamental definition

**Shared Understanding is the living, deliberately-incomplete account of what has become clear enough about a piece of work to be relied upon — held at its honest epistemic status, with its authorship legible, together with the frontier of what is still open.**

Three things must be distinguished, because conflating them is the deepest error available here:

- **The operator's understanding** — what the operator actually grasps. Private, sovereign, and *the only thing that ultimately matters*. It is the point of the entire exercise.
- **Director's Read** — Director's private, continuously-updating model of the situation (from the Leadership Intelligence Model). Also private; it is how Director thinks, not a shared truth.
- **The Shared Understanding** — the *external, legible, mutually-relied-upon account* that bridges the two. It is what the operator and Director can both point at, test against, and depend on.

The Shared Understanding is **the bridge**, not a third mind. This resolves the tension the brief rightly insists we face honestly — *can understanding truly be shared between a human and a system?* No, not as cognition. A system does not "understand" the way a person does, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. What is shared is not the understanding-in-the-head; it is a **common reference** that both parties have made reliable and both agree to treat as current truth. "Shared" means *mutually relied upon and mutually legible*. The understanding remains the operator's; the Shared Understanding is its externalized, agreed reflection. Director's contribution is to help make the operator's understanding strong enough to externalize, and to keep the external record honest.

**Is it an artifact, a state, a relationship, or a product surface?** It is a **state**, expressed through a durable record, in service of a relationship. Precisely: it is a *reliance state* — the current standing of what can be depended on — and its record is what the earlier doctrine called Shared Understanding made concrete. If a sharper internal name helps builders keep it honest, the technical essence is a **reliance surface**: the set of claims the work currently rests on, at their true epistemic status, plus the frontier of what's still open. "Shared Understanding" is the relationship-level name; "reliance surface" is what it *is*. We keep the term, having defined it precisely — but note that "the Understanding of Record" or "reliance surface" are its honest cores, and if the word "shared" ever tempts anyone toward "the system understands," they should reach for those instead.

**A founding principle falls out immediately: the record is deliberately incomplete.** It captures what has become *load-bearing* or *relied-upon*, plus the honest frontier — not everything true, not everything said. Completeness is an anti-goal. A Shared Understanding that tried to record every fact mentioned would become a database, a transcript, a form — exactly the things the brief says it must not be. Its incompleteness is a feature: it is a *reliance surface*, and only load-bearing things earn a place on it.

## 2. The content model — the smallest coherent core

The brief lists more than twenty candidate dimensions and asks for the *smallest coherent model*, not a template. The temptation is a flat form with twenty fields. Resist it. Almost all of those dimensions are the **same primitive** in different states.

**There is one primitive: the *claim*.** A claim is a statement about the work, and it carries two properties that define everything: its **epistemic status** (how sure, what kind) and its **authorship** (who established it). Watch the twenty dimensions collapse:

- A **fact** is a claim established as *known*.
- An **assumption** is a claim *relied on but unverified*.
- A **decision** is a claim the operator has *committed to*.
- A **recommendation** is a claim Director *advises* but the operator has not committed to.
- A **risk** is a claim about a *possible bad future*.
- An **unknown** is a claim-shaped *hole* — a question whose answer would matter.
- A **constraint** is a claim that *bounds* the space.
- An **accepted imperfection** is a decision made in known, recorded acceptance of its downside.
- **Alternatives** and **tradeoffs** are *related claims about options*.
- **Prior learning** is an *imported claim* carrying its origin.

They are not different fields. They are one thing — a claim — typed by *epistemic status × authorship*. That single insight is what makes a genuinely small model able to represent serious engineering work without a template.

Around that primitive, the *fundamental* dimensions are only these:

1. **Intent** — what we are trying to achieve, and why. The anchor. Every claim exists in service of it; without intent, no claim has meaning. Intent is itself a claim, but a privileged, operator-authored one — the thing the rest is *for*.
2. **The body of claims** — everything above, each at its honest status and authorship. This is the substance.
3. **The frontier** — a *derived* view: the claims that are open, contested, or load-bearing-and-uncertain, and specifically **what would change the current direction**. The frontier is not separate content; it is the salient edge of the claim body, and keeping it visible is one of the model's most important jobs.
4. **Provenance** — the history of how claims reached their current state: what changed, and why. This is what lets anyone answer "what changed?"

Everything else on the list is a **view** over the claim body, not a fundamental dimension:

- **Scope** is the set of claims marked in / out.
- **Sequencing** is an ordering over claims.
- **Acceptance** is the set of claims that define "done."
- **Current situation / existing capability / architecture / prior work** are claims of kind *fact* or *imported*.
- **Confidence** is a property of a claim's status (§3), not its own field.
- **Execution approach** is a set of claims, mostly left open for the worker (§10).

So the minimum coherent model is: **Intent, a body of claims (typed by status × authorship), the frontier, and provenance.** Four things, one of which (the claim) does almost all the work. That is small enough to maintain and rich enough to hold real engineering — the two properties a template can never have at once.

## 3. Epistemic status — how the model tells kinds of claims apart

A claim's *status* is how the model stays honest about what is actually known versus wished. The legitimate states:

- **Known** — established; we can rely on it.
- **Believed** — held with reason, not yet established. (Carries a qualitative strength — see below.)
- **Assumed** — relied on without verification, consciously.
- **Proposed** — put forward (often by Director), not yet accepted.
- **Decided** — the operator has committed to it.
- **Contested** — two positions are held, both preserved (see disagreement, below).
- **Unknown** — a named hole; we know we don't know.
- **Deferred** — a known unknown we have consciously chosen to proceed *without* resolving.
- **Superseded** — was relied upon, no longer is; retained in history.
- **Accepted-as-imperfect** — decided in full, recorded knowledge that it isn't optimal.

**Confidence without scoring.** Confidence is not a number attached to a claim; it is *the strength of a status, expressed in the language of its evidence*, per the Constitution (confidence ∝ evidence). "Believed, strongly, because two independent sources agree" and "believed, tentatively, on one person's recollection" are different states, distinguished by their *reason*, not by 0.8 vs 0.4. Numerical scores manufacture false precision and invite gamification; the model refuses them. Confidence lives in the *why*, and the why is always visible. A claim that cannot state why it is believed is, by that fact, weaker — and the model shows that honestly.

**Disagreement is a state, not an error.** When the operator and Director (or two lines of reasoning) hold different positions, the claim is **contested**, and *both positions are preserved with their authorship and reasoning*. Contested is stable and legitimate — some things stay contested straight through execution, and that is fine as long as the disagreement is *visible* and the operator has chosen the path knowingly. The model never resolves a contest by quietly picking a side; only the operator resolves it, by deciding, and even then Director's dissent is retained in history as "advised otherwise, here's why."

**Ambiguity is retained, not collapsed.** *Unknown* and *deferred* are first-class. The model's most important discipline is that it will **never convert an unknown into a false decision to look complete.** A deferred unknown — "we know we don't know X, and we've chosen to proceed anyway" — is a *decision about an unknown*, and it is one of the most honest things a Shared Understanding can contain. Premature resolution to reduce visible openness is the failure this section exists to prevent.

## 4. Authorship and sovereignty

Every claim carries *who established it*, and this is what structurally preserves the operator's sovereignty. The authorship kinds:

- **Operator-decided** — the operator committed to it. Only the operator can produce this.
- **Director-advised** — Director recommends it; it remains advice until the operator decides.
- **Jointly-found** — a fact established together through the work.
- **Imported** — prior knowledge brought in, carrying its origin.
- **Contested** — held differently by different authors, all preserved.

Two structural rules make sovereignty real rather than declared:

1. **Director cannot author a relied-upon claim.** Director may add claims only in *non-authoritative* states — proposed, advised, a named risk, a named unknown. Only the operator can move a claim into a *relied-upon, operator-authored* state (decided, accepted). This is how "advice never silently becomes truth" is guaranteed structurally, not by good manners: a recommendation *cannot* become a decision without the operator's commitment, at which point authorship flips to the operator and the recommendation is retained as its *rationale*. Director's advice becoming a decision is always an operator act, and always visible as one.
2. **Director cannot rewrite intent.** Intent is operator-authored. Director may *question* it — surface that the intent seems to be drifting, or that a claim conflicts with it — but it may never edit intent to match its own recommendation. The most subtle way an advisor overrides a principal is by quietly reshaping the goal; the model forbids it.

**Legible, not bureaucratic.** Authorship is carried in *voice*, not in form fields. The difference between "you decided X," "I'd advise Y," "we found Z," and "still open" is grammatical and ambient — the same way a good colleague's speech naturally tells you what's settled versus suggested versus unknown, without anyone filling in an "author" column. Attribution is surfaced *where it matters* — on decisions and advice and disagreements — and is invisible for ordinary facts. If authorship ever feels like paperwork, the model is being applied wrong (§11).

## 5. Evolution over time

A Shared Understanding is thin at the start and thickens through discovery. Its shape at each stage:

- **Thin (arrival).** Mostly *intent*, a few *facts* about the situation, and many *unknowns* and *proposed* directions. Honest thinness is correct here; the worst early failure is a Shared Understanding that looks confident before anything is actually known. Early, the frontier is almost everything.
- **Thickening (discovery).** Unknowns resolve into facts or decisions; assumptions get tested — confirmed (→ known) or falsified (→ revise); prior work is imported and re-tested against the current situation; contested points appear and are held honestly. The frontier shrinks as load-bearing claims firm up.
- **Firm (convergence).** The load-bearing claims are reliable; a few imperfections are consciously accepted; the frontier has narrowed to things that won't invalidate the direction (§6).

**Revisions are status changes with retained history.** A claim moves — *proposed → decided*, *believed → contested*, *decided → superseded* — and its prior state is **kept in history, never deleted**. The Shared Understanding therefore has two layers: the **current reliance surface** (what stands *now*) and the **history** (how it got here, what we used to think, why it changed). This is how superseded understanding "remains available without polluting the current state": it is demoted out of the reliance surface but preserved in history, reachable when someone asks "didn't we decide differently before?" — answerable with "yes, and here's why it changed."

**Durability.** A decision becomes durable not by being marked decided but by being *committed by the operator and then surviving* — accumulating a track record of not needing to reopen. Durability is earned by stability, the same way trust is.

**Reopening — and protecting from it.** A settled decision is reopened **only on new information that bears on it** (per the Constitution). Reopening a settled decision without new cause is a trust cost and an attention cost, and Director actively *protects* decisions from casual reopening — it will decline to relitigate something the operator settled, unless a new claim genuinely challenges it. But "settled" resists *casual* reopening, never *warranted* reopening: when the ground truly shifts, reopening is not just allowed but obligatory, and Director raises it — once, clearly, with the new information that justifies it. The asymmetry is the point: hard to reopen on a whim, easy to reopen on evidence.

## 6. Convergence

Convergence is the most misunderstood word in the model, so define it by what it is *not*: it does **not** mean every question is answered, Director agrees, the record is complete, confidence is high everywhere, or all risks are gone. A Shared Understanding can be fully execution-worthy with open unknowns, accepted imperfections, and even a live contest.

The concept that makes convergence coherent is **load-bearing claims**: the small set of claims whose falsity would *collapse the direction*. Not every claim matters equally; a handful carry the weight. Convergence is entirely about that subset.

- **Healthy convergence** — the *load-bearing* claims have become reliable enough to act on, the operator understands and owns them, and the remaining openness is either non-load-bearing or consciously accepted. The frontier has shrunk to things that *won't* invalidate the direction. This is earned narrowing.
- **Premature convergence** — the frontier was collapsed before the load-bearing claims were actually reliable. The record *looks* settled; the reasoning underneath isn't there. Deciding to feel done.
- **False convergence** — apparent agreement with no reliability under it: claims marked decided that no one can defend, contests quietly papered over. Consensus theater. The most dangerous, because it hides.
- **Unresolved-but-execution-worthy** — open unknowns and even contested claims remain, but the open items are *not load-bearing*, or are *consciously deferred/accepted*. You can execute on this honestly, because you know exactly what you're betting on and what you're leaving open.

**Execution readiness** therefore is not "everything resolved." It is: **the load-bearing claims are reliable enough to act on, the operator understands and owns them, and every remaining risk, unknown, and imperfection is visible and consciously accepted.** Readiness is a property of the load-bearing subset plus honesty about the rest — and, per the Constitution, it lives in the operator's understanding, not in the record's completeness. The record can *inform* readiness; only the operator can *accept* it.

## 7. Mission Health, redefined

Mission Health was previously called an instrument, not the truth. This model fixes its role precisely, and narrows it hard, because a health metric is one bad step from becoming a progress bar.

**Mission Health is a diagnosis of the frontier, in plain language: "what here is safe to rely on, and what isn't yet — and why."** Nothing more. It reads the load-bearing claims and the honesty of the frontier, and it names the weak, load-bearing spots. Its output is not "72% ready" and not a checklist of green ticks; it is *"these two things are what I'd want firmer before we act, and here's why."* If that is clearer under a different name, call it **the Reliance Read** — a reading of what can and cannot yet be relied upon. The name matters less than the discipline.

- **What it assesses:** all three — the work, the reasoning, and (indirectly) the operator's understanding — but through a single lens: *is this strong enough to rely on, and does the operator know what they're relying on?* It points attention at the weak load-bearing claims.
- **What it is useful for:** orienting the operator to where the understanding is thin *where it matters*, and informing (never making) their readiness judgment.
- **What it must never do:** produce a progress score, a checklist, a gamified percentage, false precision, or a Ready/Not-Ready badge. It must never *claim* readiness — readiness is the operator's to accept. Health *informs*; the operator *decides*. The moment Mission Health becomes a number the operator chases, it has become the thing it was built to replace.

## 8. How Leadership Intelligence changes the Shared Understanding

The Leadership Intelligence Model's loop — observation → signal → Read → available move → intervention or silence — ends at the Shared Understanding, but the simple linear sequence *undersells* how change actually happens. There are several paths, and Director's power on each is deliberately limited:

- **Operator-driven change.** The operator decides or discovers; the Shared Understanding changes directly. Director only observes, and its Read updates. Most change is this. Director does not mediate the operator's own thinking into the record.
- **Intervention-driven change.** A Director move causes the operator to add, examine, or revise a claim — but Director's move only ever *proposes*; the operator's response is what actually changes the reliance surface.
- **Silent preservation.** Director's *silence* is also an act on the Shared Understanding — by *not* reopening a settled decision, it protects the surface's stability; by *holding* a signal, it avoids destabilizing a sound direction. Silence can preserve understanding as surely as speech can change it. This is invisible in a linear diagram and essential in practice.

Which changes require what:

- **Silent (no operator action):** Director's Read updates from observation. Nothing enters the reliance surface. This is most of Director's activity.
- **Director-proposed (enters only as non-authoritative):** Director may *add* a claim to the Shared Understanding, but only in a non-authoritative state — a named risk, a named unknown, a proposed direction, an advised recommendation. A proposed claim is *visible but not relied-upon*.
- **Requires operator acknowledgment:** a proposed claim becomes part of what the operator is *working from* only once they register it. Director cannot make the operator's understanding change; it can only offer.
- **Requires an explicit operator decision:** any transition into a *relied-upon, operator-authored* state — decided, accepted, deferred. Only the operator.

The governing rule, restating §4 operationally: **Director may suggest, name, flag, and advise — it may never commit.** Every authoritative change to the reliance surface is an operator act. This is what keeps the Shared Understanding from becoming Director's verdict wearing the operator's name.

## 9. Memory and continuity

The Shared Understanding is where engineering memory becomes usable — but memory that enters carelessly becomes dogma, and memory that never fades becomes noise.

**Prior work enters as re-testable imported claims.** When past work is relevant, it enters the Shared Understanding *authored as imported*, carrying its original context, and — crucially — **not automatically relied upon**. It enters as *believed* or *proposed*, subject to re-testing against the current situation. Precedent *informs*; it does not *bind*. Director offers precedent ("this resembles X; here's what we learned there"); the operator decides its relevance.

**Distinguishing kinds of precedent is a judgment, surfaced as advice** — never an automatic import, because the distinctions are exactly where pattern-matching goes wrong:

- **Relevant precedent** — the context still holds; lean on it.
- **Outdated precedent** — the context changed; the old conclusion may no longer apply.
- **Failed precedent** — this was tried and didn't work; a warning, carried so it isn't retried.
- **Reusable implementation** — actual work to build on, not just a lesson.
- **Repeated mistake** — "this is the third time we've hit this"; the memory that prevents a loop.
- **Superficially similar but materially different** — the most dangerous kind. Here Director's job is to flag the *difference*, not the similarity, because the resemblance is a trap. A model that only surfaced "this looks like X" would actively mislead; the value is in "this looks like X *but differs in the way that matters*."

**What is kept permanently** (the durable memory of a capability): decisions and their rationale, accepted imperfections, failed approaches and *why* they failed, and load-bearing constraints. These persist across engagements — they are the accumulated engineering judgment of the capability.

**What stays local** to one engagement: the discovery scaffolding, transient assumptions, intermediate reasoning — the working notes that mattered *while* forming the understanding but aren't load-bearing afterward.

**What should fade:** superseded claims (kept in history but demoted), stale context, resolved unknowns that no longer bear on anything. Forgetting is a feature. Carrying every past detail forward is precisely how memory becomes the noise the Constitution warns against. The principle: **remember the load-bearing and the learned; let the scaffolding fade.**

**A reframe this forces — the durable unit is not the mission.** If memory persists across engagements and the understanding continues through execution and beyond, then the "mission" (a bounded task with a start and end) is not the primary unit. The durable unit is **the capability's Shared Understanding**, which is long-lived; a *mission is an episode that advances it*. Each mission thickens the capability's understanding and leaves it stronger. This is the better unit the brief invited: the Shared Understanding of a capability is what persists; missions are how it grows.

## 10. Handoff to execution

Describe the *human meaning* of the handoff, not a package. When execution begins, the worker does not receive a specification to obey. They inherit a **shared understanding to continue** — the same reliance surface, entering a new phase.

**What must be understood before execution:**
- The **intent** — what we're achieving and why (without it, every instruction is brittle).
- The **decided claims** — what's settled, and the reasoning under each (so the worker knows what *not* to reopen, and why).
- The **load-bearing assumptions** — what we're betting on. These are the most important thing to hand over, because the worker's job includes *watching for them to break*.
- The **visible risks and accepted imperfections** — so nothing surprises them, and so they don't "fix" an imperfection that was consciously accepted.
- The **acceptance** — what "done" means here.

**What uncertainty can remain:** plenty. Non-load-bearing unknowns and consciously-deferred items ride along openly. The worker inherits an *honest frontier*, not a false-complete spec. A handoff that pretends to certainty it doesn't have sets the worker up to be blindsided.

**What freedom the worker retains:** everything not decided. The Shared Understanding says what is settled *and why*, and deliberately leaves the rest to the worker's engineering judgment. Over-specifying steals the worker's craft and is itself an engineering failure — the model hands over decisions and their reasons, not a script.

**Execution discoveries flow back.** This is the crucial one: **execution does not end the understanding process.** What the worker discovers — a fact, a broken assumption, a new constraint — are new observations that update the Shared Understanding. A discovery that *falsifies a load-bearing assumption* reopens the reliance surface and returns the work to counsel. The handoff is therefore **not a wall; it is a phase transition** within one continuous understanding. Discovery, decision, execution, and reflection are phases of the same engagement, over the same Shared Understanding — which is exactly why *one* model can represent both discovery and execution: they are not two things.

## 11. Failure modes

The most dangerous ways this product can go wrong. The brief named many; the deeper ones matter most.

- **It becomes a form.** The reliance surface turns into fields to fill. *Guard:* one primitive (the claim), deliberate incompleteness, authorship carried in voice not columns.
- **Director over-documents.** Everything gets promoted to the record. *Guard:* promotion is *earned* — a claim enters only when it becomes load-bearing or relied-upon, the same discipline as intervention. Most thinking stays in the Read and the conversation and is never written down.
- **Everything becomes permanent.** Every statement ossifies. *Guard:* current reliance surface vs. history; superseded claims demote; scaffolding fades.
- **Recommendations quietly become decisions.** The advice-to-truth leak — the single most corrosive failure. *Guard:* the structural rule — Director cannot author a relied-upon claim; only an operator decision flips advice into a decision, visibly.
- **The operator stops thinking because the state looks authoritative.** The record becomes an oracle; understanding leaves the operator's head. *Guard:* the frontier and the uncertainty stay *prominent*; a healthy Shared Understanding makes the operator *more* engaged with what's unresolved, not less. The record is never authoritative over the operator's actual understanding — if they diverge, the operator's mind wins and the record is corrected.
- **Old decisions prevent necessary change.** Ossification. *Guard:* reopening on new information is legitimate and easy; "settled" resists caprice, not evidence.
- **False consensus.** Contests get quietly marked decided to look converged. *Guard:* contested is a first-class, visible, stable state; disagreement is never hidden to manufacture agreement.
- **Nuance compressed into status labels.** The richness collapses into badges. *Guard:* confidence lives in the *why*, not a score; contested carries both positions and their reasoning; no numeric health.
- **Maintenance exceeds the engineering.** Keeping the understanding current costs more than doing the work. *Guard:* the sharpest tell that the model is misapplied — if maintaining it feels like more work than engineering, it is capturing too much. It should feel like a by-product, not a chore.
- **Director optimizes the artifact instead of the operator.** *Guard:* the whole Constitution — the product is the operator's understanding; the record serves it and never the reverse.

Deeper failure modes worth naming explicitly:

- **The frontier disappears.** As claims get decided, the record stops showing *what could still invalidate the direction*, breeding quiet overconfidence at exactly the moment stakes are highest. *Guard:* the frontier — and specifically the load-bearing assumptions and "what would change our mind" — must remain visible *even at high convergence*. A Shared Understanding that can't tell you how it could be wrong is not strong; it is blind.
- **Premature durability.** A claim is marked *decided* too early and then treated as settled, foreclosing the exploration that should have continued. *Guard:* the exploration phase keeps claims *proposed/believed* longer; *decided* requires genuine operator commitment, not momentum.
- **Understanding drift.** The record and the operator's actual grasp diverge, and the "shared" silently breaks. *Guard:* the record is only ever a reflection the operator recognizes as theirs; the model's health depends on the operator being able to look at it and say "yes, that's what I think" — and correct it the moment they can't.

## 12. The experience

Without designing an interface, define the feel. Working with a Shared Understanding should feel like the work having a *memory and a conscience that you don't have to maintain*. The operator should be able to answer, at any moment and almost without looking:

- *What are we actually doing, and why?* — the intent is always present.
- *What do we currently believe, and how firmly?* — claims at honest status, confidence in the why.
- *What have we decided, and what's still open?* — the reliance surface and the frontier, cleanly distinct.
- *What changed, and why?* — provenance, one glance away.
- *What is Director recommending, and where is it uncertain?* — advice legible as advice, uncertainty named.
- *What could still invalidate this direction?* — the load-bearing assumptions, never hidden.
- *Are we ready to execute, and why?* — a reliance judgment the operator makes, informed but not made for them.

The defining quality is that these answers feel **ambient, not administratively maintained** — discovered, not filed. The operator should never feel they are *keeping the Shared Understanding up to date*; it should feel like the natural residue of thinking well, the way a good engineer's mental model of their system feels *there* without being *maintained*. The instant it feels like documentation, the product has failed, no matter how complete the documentation is.

---

## Product principles

1. **The record captures what is *relied upon*, not what is *true*.** Deliberate incompleteness is a feature.
2. **Understanding lives in the operator's mind.** The Shared Understanding is its externalized, mutually-relied-upon reflection — never its replacement, never its authority.
3. **One primitive: the claim, typed by epistemic status × authorship.** Everything else is a view.
4. **Director may suggest, name, flag, and advise — never commit.** Only an operator decision makes a claim relied-upon. Advice never silently becomes truth.
5. **Ambiguity is retained honestly.** Unknown, contested, deferred, and accepted-as-imperfect are first-class, stable states.
6. **The frontier stays visible** — the Shared Understanding always knows, and shows, how it could still be wrong.
7. **Convergence is about the load-bearing subset**, not completeness or agreement.
8. **Current reliance surface and history are separate.** Superseded understanding is retained, demoted, never deleted, never polluting the present.
9. **Memory keeps the load-bearing and the learned; the scaffolding fades.**
10. **Execution continues the understanding.** The handoff is a phase transition, not a wall.
11. **The capability's Shared Understanding is the durable unit; a mission is an episode that advances it.**
12. **If maintaining it feels like more work than the engineering, it is capturing too much.**

## Worked example — one understanding over time

*Following a single capability's Shared Understanding across a mission and into execution. (Illustrative, to show the model changing — not a template.)*

**Thin (arrival).**
Intent (operator): *make scheduling honest about which children a room can actually take.* A few facts imported about the current scheduler. Almost everything else is **unknown**: how age is evaluated, whether ratios are hard limits, what "a room" even means here. The frontier is nearly the whole thing. Mission Health reads: *"We don't yet know the two things this rests on — how age is evaluated, and whether ratios are hard gates. Everything else is premature until those firm up."*

**Thickening (discovery).**
Director surfaces a **contested** point: it *advises* that ratios be treated as hard gates (Director-advised, proposed); the operator initially disagrees. Held as contested, both positions preserved. An **assumption** — "age is evaluated as of today's date" — is tested against a real case and *falsified*; it becomes a revised claim: age must be evaluated *as of the effective date* (jointly-found, now **known**). An **imported** precedent from a prior placement attempt is offered; Director flags it as *superficially similar but materially different* — same shape, different age boundaries — so it informs without binding. The operator **decides** ratios are hard gates; the contest resolves, Director's prior agreement now underpins it, and the earlier disagreement is retained in history.

**Firm (convergence).**
The two **load-bearing** claims — age-as-of-effective-date, ratios-as-hard-gates — are now *known* and *decided*, owned by the operator, each with its reasoning. One **accepted imperfection** is recorded: the room-fit check won't consider mid-year birthdays shifting a child's band — known, accepted, so no one "fixes" it later. The frontier has shrunk to non-load-bearing unknowns. Mission Health: *"The two things this rests on are settled and sound. What's open won't change the direction. Your call on readiness."* The operator accepts readiness. Note what convergence did *not* require: a live minor question remains open, and that's fine.

**Handoff (execution begins).**
The worker inherits: the intent, the two decisions *and their reasoning*, the load-bearing assumptions (evaluate age as of the effective date; ratios are hard), the accepted imperfection (so they don't "fix" it), and freedom over everything else — how to structure the check is theirs.

**Execution discovery (the loop closes).**
Mid-build, the worker discovers a case where "the effective date" is itself ambiguous for waitlisted children — a fact that *bears on a load-bearing assumption*. It flows back as a new observation; the reliance surface **reopens** on that one claim; the work briefly returns to counsel to settle it; then execution continues. The understanding did not end at handoff. It continued through it.

## The tests every future product decision must pass

1. **Does it keep understanding in the operator's mind, or migrate it into the artifact?** (Must keep it in the operator.)
2. **Does it preserve the distinctions — fact / interpretation / assumption / decision / recommendation / risk / unknown / constraint / intent / prior-learning / accepted-imperfection — or flatten them?** (Must preserve.)
3. **Can Director's advice become a relied-upon truth without an explicit operator decision?** (Must be impossible.)
4. **Does the frontier — how this could still be wrong — stay visible, even at high convergence?** (Must stay visible.)
5. **Does it retain ambiguity honestly, or collapse it to look complete?** (Must retain.)
6. **Does maintaining it cost less than the engineering it supports?** (Must — or it's capturing too much.)
7. **Does superseded understanding stay available without polluting the current reliance surface?** (Must.)
8. **Does it treat the capability's understanding as the durable unit and the mission as an episode?** (Must.)
9. **Does it let the operator answer the §12 questions ambiently, without feeling they maintain a document?** (Must.)
10. **Does it optimize the operator's understanding, or the artifact?** (Must be the operator's understanding — always.)

A product decision that fails any of these is wrong, however useful, complete, or requested — for the same reason a feature that violates the Constitution is wrong. This model governs every conversation, intervention, mission, review, and handoff, in service of a single truth: *Director does not manufacture an authoritative package. Director helps the operator build, challenge, revise, and eventually rely upon a sufficiently strong understanding of the work.*
