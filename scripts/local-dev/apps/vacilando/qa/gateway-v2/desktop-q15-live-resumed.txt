## Decision

**Access is the canonical home for capability configuration.**

Capabilities are configured and understood centrally through the Access product.

Individual operational/configuration surfaces **consume effective capability authority** to determine visibility and permitted behavior, but they do not independently own or redefine capability configuration.

Do not distribute capability configuration across Enrollment, Communications, Roster, Processing, Billing, Settings subsections, or other individual product surfaces.

This preserves the accepted four-layer Access model:

1. Membership
2. Role
3. Capability
4. Scope

and keeps authorization configuration understandable from one canonical Access product.

## Important distinction

This does **not** mean Access owns the business capability itself.

The platform/domain still owns what a capability means and server enforcement remains authoritative.

Access owns the operator experience for:

* assigning roles;
* understanding role capability grants;
* configuring permitted capability grants where tenant configuration allows it;
* understanding effective scope;
* explaining effective access.

Operational surfaces consume that authority.

Surface visibility remains a projection of capability authority — never the enforcement mechanism itself.

## Product objective

Move `/organization/access` from a truthful-but-still-technical authority surface toward the **premium operator-facing Access product originally intended**.

Do not create a new route or separate Identity application.

The existing `/organization/access` product should converge around the four-layer mental model.

The operator should be able to answer:

* Who has access?
* What role do they have?
* What can that role do?
* Where does that authority apply?
* Why does this user have this access?

without understanding database tables, permission keys, resolver internals, or legacy authority stores.
